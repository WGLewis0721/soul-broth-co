Now serving

Two kitchens,
one bowl.

The broth starts the way a pot of greens does — oxtail, smoked turkey, aromatics, a whole afternoon. Then it gets a ramen shop's discipline: soy tare measured to the gram, noodles pulled to order, toppings stacked with intent.

It's not Southern food dressed up as ramen, or ramen borrowing a few Southern notes. It's one cook's two grandmothers arguing in the same pot, and somehow agreeing.

“If it doesn't stick to your ribs, we're not sending it out the window.”
